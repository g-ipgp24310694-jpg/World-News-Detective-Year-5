    Academic integrity reminder

    This website is a draft framework. Revise its scripts, questions, visuals and content yourself. Record your own narration before submitting your final product, as required by your lecturer.

    01 / TEACHER GUIDE

    30-minute listening lesson

    1. Pre-listening · 5 minPictures, topic prediction and optional vocabulary clues.
    2. While-listening · 15 minPlay each report twice. Pupils complete each passport, then play Sound Hunt or choose one optional challenge.
    3. Post-listening · 10 minChoose one of: six-question quiz, Story Scramble or Fact Check. Compare perspectives in pairs and complete the exit ticket.

    Suggested ABCD objectives

    Given two short audio reports and an interactive passport, Year 5 pupils will identify the country, day, place and event with at least 3 of 4 correct for each report.

    After listening to two reports and discussing with a partner, Year 5 pupils will state one accurate similarity, one accurate difference and one respectful question, using optional sentence frames.

    30-minute lesson: Choose one bonus game, not all of them. Extra games are for extension, differentiation or a longer showcase.

    Report A: Culture Sharing Day (Malaysia)

    Good morning, young detectives!

    Welcome to Young Voices News.

    Our first story comes from Sarawak, Malaysia.

    On Thursday, Year Five pupils at Ria Primary School held a Culture Sharing Day in the school hall.

    Families brought photographs and taught traditional games.

    Pupils shared stories about Gawai, Hari Raya, and Chinese New Year.

    After the event, pupils made thank-you cards in English.

    Their teacher, Miss Lim, said, “We learn when we listen to each other.”

    That is our first news report!

    Did you hear all the important clues?

    Report B: Family Stories Day (New Zealand)

    Hello again, detectives!

    This is Young Voices News.

    Our second story comes from Wellington, New Zealand.

    On Friday, pupils at Harbour Primary School held a Family Stories Day in the library.

    They showed photographs, shared family stories, and taught one another greetings in different languages.

    After the event, teams made colourful posters about respecting other traditions.

    A pupil named Ruby said, “Every family has a story worth hearing.”

    And that is our second news report!

    Now, detectives, what do these two schools have in common?

    04 / DESIGN JUSTIFICATION

    Why this material is learner-centred

    Autonomy & self-monitoring

    Pupils choose the order, replay reports and check their own responses.

    Scaffolding

    Optional word clues, short reports, repeated listening and sentence frames support access.

    Co-learning & interaction

    Pupils compare responses and discuss cultural observations with a partner.

    Intercultural competence

    Pupils notice similarities and differences without generalising about entire cultures.

    Academic evidence: Add and verify your own 2020–2026 citations in the presentation or accompanying report. Avoid presenting these rationale statements as sourced quotations.

    05 / OPTIONS FOR DIFFERENT LEARNERS

    Choose a suitable learning path

    More support

    Preview pictures, allow repeated listening, open optional word clues and complete a passport with a partner.

    Core mission

    Predict → listen twice → passport → Sound Hunt → partner comparison.

    Extra challenge

    Try Fact Check or Story Scramble without reading a transcript, then explain an answer using audio evidence.

    Respectful talk

    Discuss one similarity and one difference without saying every person in a country is the same.

    06 / SITE PERSONALISATION

    Make it yours

    • Revise all fictional names and reports so they reflect your intended pupils and learner profile.
    • Record and publish your own audio files in assets/audio/; use the scripts only as drafts.
    • Change the colours, wording and visual elements yourself; remove activities that do not suit your pupils.
    • Test on a phone and laptop, with a teacher-led offline alternative.
    • Never ask pupils for real names, IDs, passwords or sensitive information.
